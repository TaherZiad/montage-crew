# MG recipes — the target level

Two frame-measured teardowns of target-level reference reels, plus the recipes built from them. They are the quality bar for motion graphics: every number below was measured from real frames (60p), not guessed. Pick **one palette system per video**; never mix the two accents.

- **System A (coral 3-colour metaphor)**: target-level reference reel A, a 1080×1920, 60 fps, 46.2 s pure-MG Arabic piece. One visual metaphor per clause (26 in 46 s), one heavy centred Arabic headline with one accent word, a strict 3-colour world (charcoal / cream / coral), designed 6-10 f transitions or in-scene morphs between metaphors.
- **System B (dark HUD + orange)**: target-level reference reel B, a 97.8 s landscape English MG explainer. Near-black stage, one hot-orange accent, glow, 2.5D camera, heat-cool word reveals. Recipes MG-201 … MG-221.

Notes: both reels were analysed for picture only (audio ignored). Reel A's on-screen words are given as English glosses of the MSA original (in quotes); the timings, sizes and positions apply to any Arabic word of similar length. Product names on screen are replaced by the placeholder `Nova 5.5`.

# System A (coral 3-colour metaphor)

## How to use it in talking-head reels (adaptation, not copy)
1. **Metaphor per sentence**: for every idea ask "what object IS this sentence?" (film strip = every frame; brick wall = old editing timeline; prompt bar -> city rising = one sentence builds a site; one line drawing = "one stroke"; letter folding/dancing = "I bend the letter"; ECG + counter = "life in numbers"; waveform -> radial sun = "sound gets a shape"; particles -> one dot = "all this started from one idea"; pause icon glitch = "wait"; struck word = negation; fingerprint drawn live = "your own way"; pedestal = "your place"; two racing lines = "who masters it gets ahead"). Build that object BIG on the stage (split panel bottom half, or a 1-2.5 s full-frame cutaway), never as small cards.
2. **Full-frame MG cutaways** of 1.1-3 s between talking shots are allowed for the strongest 2-4 ideas; the speaker stays on screen the rest of the time (split stage).
3. **Palette discipline**: 3 colours per video (dark / light / one accent). Text always in the two colours that are not the background. Flip the stage background between beats (never the same twice in a row).
4. **Headline system** (only on cutaways/stage, never duplicating the caption words on screen at the same time): one centred heavy Arabic line, words pop RTL at their final positions 18-36 f apart, accent word = colour only.
5. **Transitions are designed and short**: wipe with a 24 px edge bar in another palette colour (quart in-out, 6-9 f), glitch-cut (6-7 f slices), iris grown from a UI element (caret, spark, strike line), push into colour, crossfade matched by position, and above all MORPH the old object into the next (cell->card, pill->city, line->3D tube, wave->sun, particles->dot, ring->token). Max 1 plain hard cut.
6. **Never fully still**: slow drift (grid 40 px/s, particles, ticking counter); a frame counter `f ####` as a tech micro-detail fits AI/coding topics.
7. **Defaults for talking-head reels** (unless the brief, the profile or the user's `rules.md` says otherwise): no boxes behind captions (headline chips not used); keep the phrase captions; keep SFX few and quiet; keep zooms smooth.

### 2. Typography

**Arabic font.** None of the tested fonts is an exact match. A correct font would score above 0.85 on per-word overlap; these scored:

| Font | Per-word overlap (IoU) |
|---|---|
| Cairo 900 | 0.58 (best available) |
| Tajawal Black / ExtraBold | 0.56 |
| Cairo 800 | 0.55 |
| Alexandria 800 | 0.49 |
| IBM Plex Sans Arabic Bold | 0.39 (rejected) |

Thmanyah Sans, Readex Pro, Rubik and Noto Kufi were not tested. The best candidate is a heavy, low-contrast, geometric Naskh-style sans:
- flat cut terminals and round dots;
- the initial kaf has a diagonal ascender;
- wide, open counters;
- short ascenders: alif about 1.7× the x-height.

For our builds, **Cairo 800–900 or Tajawal ExtraBold** is the closest stand-in.

**Weight and size (measured):**
- Baseline stroke 14–18 px; x-height 44–53 px; alif about 85 px; descender 25–30 px below the baseline.
- The equivalent font size, back-calculated from line widths, is about 105 px in Cairo (96–116 px), roughly 10 % of the frame width.
- A single weight is used throughout; there is no light/heavy pairing.

**Latin font.** The product name (placeholder `Nova 5.5`) is Inter ExtraBold/Black. It is set with tight tracking, about −5 %; the reference is 10 % narrower than Inter's default spacing.
- End card: "O" cap height 114 px (about 152 px font size), baseline y≈1040.
- The account handle under it is about 36 px tall, Inter SemiBold, colour `#7F7B75`.

**Line placement:**
- Headline baselines sit at y≈269–331, mostly 299–321 (16 % of the height). Ascender tops are at y≈188–231.
- Two-line spacing, baseline to baseline: 138 px (2590) and 150 px (2390), about 1.35× the font size.
- The second, bottom caption line has the same size, with its baseline at y≈1400–1470.

**Alignment.** Every settled line is centred: the measured line centre is x=538–540 in 60+ frames. Words appear right to left at their final positions in the centred line, so nothing reflows. For example, "I draw" alone sits at x 690–967 at frame 780.
- The widest line spans x≈97–983, leaving a margin of about 95 px, which is one grid cell.

**Accent word.** Same font, size and weight; only the colour changes (coral, or cream on coral). There are 1–2 accent words per line, usually the noun or verb that carries the meaning ("frame", "a city", "with a line", "life", "masters it...", "will beat you").
- Exceptions: "its sound" and "and takes it!" are set bigger, on their own line and rotated as they enter. "to editing" and "goodbye" get a coral strike-through.

**Punctuation:**
- Every clause ends with punctuation.
- The ellipsis is three separate dots (three clauses end in one).
- The comma is the Arabic comma (dot on top, tail down); four clauses end in one.
- A full stop ends two clauses, and "!" appears exactly once, on the climax word ("and takes it!").

### 3. Layout grid

**Background grid:**
- Cells are 90×90 px, with lines at x and y equal to multiples of 90 (12 columns, 21.3 rows).
- Line contrast is about 8 luma: darker on cream (−8.6), lighter on dark (+7…8). Lines are 1–2 px wide.
- The grid is present in every dark and cream beat. It is absent on coral, in the 3D city (624–767), in the particles / pause section (1688–1952) and in the opening spark (0–140).
- The grid is static (phase 0), except at 150–360, where it drifts diagonally at 0.667 px/frame (40 px/s) on both axes.

### 5. Micro-details

**Frame counter:**
- Text: lowercase "f" + 4-digit zero-padded frame number, in a bold monospace font.
- Bounding box x 122–226, y 177–200: glyphs 23 px tall (about a 30 px font), left margin 122 px.
- It ticks every frame and equals the real frame index exactly (f 0200 / 0201 / 0202 on frames 200 / 201 / 202).

- During the glitch at 146–150 it is sliced and displaced like the rest of the frame.

**Grain or noise:** none.
- The temporal standard deviation of static frames is 0.03, and inside a grid cell the high-frequency spatial deviation is 0.0–0.5.
- So the fills are perfectly clean.

**Vignette:**
- Cream and coral beats: a flat centre plateau (y≈540–1380) with falloff toward the top and bottom edges, −24 luma (about −10 %) at the edge and −28 in the corners.
- Dark beats: no vignette. Instead a warm radial glow sits behind the hero (+20 luma, `#372820`, radius about 400–500 px, reflected in its rim).

**Glow:** neon strokes have a coral core (`#D26C52`, about 10 px wide) and a soft halo extending about 30 px each side at about 20 % intensity. Comet heads and particles are cream-white (`#F0E8DD`).

**Depth and 3D:** mostly flat 2D vector work (icons, cards, waveform, ECG, rings), plus 6 rendered 3D moments: the glossy tube spark, the 3D city with fog and soft shadows, the neon tube depth beat, the 60 extrusion, the metal pedestal with a light beam, and the bokeh particles (blurred discs, i.e. fake depth of field). There are no drop shadows on 2D elements.

### 6. Motion language

**Word entrance: a "pop" spring, used everywhere.**
- Example, "life" at 1230–1241:
  - scale 0.59 → 0.79 → 0.93 → 1.13 (peak, frame 3);
  - then 1.10, 1.10, 1.08, 1.06, 1.03, 1.007, 1.00 (frame 11);
  - opacity 0 → 1 in 3 frames;
  - scaled about the word's own centre.
- "will beat you": 0.66 → 1.15 at frame 6, a small undershoot to 0.97, settled by frame 14.
- "movement": 0.37 → peak +8 % at frame 6, settled about frame 12, rising 20 px as it scales.
- So: 11–14 f total, 3–6 f to the peak, 8–15 % overshoot. That is back.out(≈2.5) or a stiff spring (damping ratio ≈0.5).
- Small words ("who", 2449) skip the scale. They appear in 1 f, with a 6 px rise and a 3 f opacity ramp.
- During the first 1–2 frames the word reads as a dim grey "ghost".

**Text and visual sync:**
- Each word reveal coincides with a visual event (a stroke reaching a point, a counter jump, an icon pop, a strike-through).
- The visual for the clause starts 0–10 f before or with the first word, and morphs on the accent word.
- The headline usually persists across a transition and is the last thing to leave: "goodbye" rides from the dark beat into the coral beat; "every frame..." persists under the film strip and the code beats.

**Ease family:** sharp in-out curves for wipes and moves; expo.in for iris growth; springs for pops. There is no linear motion except the fade through grey (−36 luma per frame).

### The 10 rules of this style

1. **Three colours only.** Backgrounds are `#181813` / `#ECECDE` / `#CF6E56`, and any text sits in the two colours that are not the background. Screen-time split about 54 / 36 / 10 % (dark / cream / coral); coral only ever comes between dark and cream; never the same colour twice in a row.
2. **Flip the background every 1.1–3.1 s** (mean about 3 s, 14 flips in 46 s). Visual beats last 66–189 f (mean 107 f / 1.8 s): one metaphor per clause, 26 in total.
3. **One centred, heavy Arabic line on a fixed top band.** Equivalent to about 105 px Cairo 800–900 (x-height about 50 px, stroke 14–18 px), baseline at y 270–330, margin ≥95 px. Line spacing 1.35× for two lines; the caption line has its baseline at y 1400–1470; nothing below y 1500.
4. **Words appear right to left at their final positions** in the centred line, with no reflow, 18–36 f apart (about 1.3 words/s).
5. **Every word pops with a spring:** scale about 0.6 → 1.13 → 1.0, opacity 0→1 in 3 f, peak at frame 3–6, settled by frame 11–14 (back.out about 2.5), scaled about the word's own centre.
6. **One accent word per line, changed by colour only.** Coral, or cream on coral; same size and weight; it is the word the visual illustrates. Strike-throughs in coral for negations.
7. **The hero is centred at (540, 900–1050)**, either full-bleed (about 1060 px) or about 550–700 px wide. It is built on a 90 px grid (lines ±8 luma) shown on dark and cream beats, hidden on coral and 3D beats.
8. **Transitions are 6–10 frames, designed, and never plain.** In this reel: 4 slide wipes with a 24 px colour edge (quart in-out, 60 % of travel in the middle 2 frames), 3 glitch-cuts, 2 iris wipes grown from a UI element (expo.in, ×1.6 radius per frame), 3 flash frames (3–4 f), plus about 10 morphs inside a scene. Exactly 1 plain hard cut.
9. **Never fully still.** Only 8 % of frames are frozen, and the longest freeze is 22 f, used once as a deliberate pause before "wait". Holds keep a slow drift (a 40 px/s grid drift, particles, a ticking counter).
10. **Clean and lit, not textured.** Zero grain (temporal deviation 0.03). On cream and coral beats, a vertical vignette of −24 luma (about −10 %) at the top and bottom edges. On dark beats, a warm radial glow `#372820` about 450 px behind the hero instead of a vignette. Neon strokes: 10 px coral core with a 30 px halo at about 20 %. A frame counter "f ####", monospace, 23 px, at x 122 / y 177, ticks with the real frame (`#7F7970` on dark, `#A75647` on cream) and freezes when the story says "pause".

## 3. Recipes (MG-2xx)

All of these are seek-safe: one paused GSAP timeline per composition, everything placed at absolute times, no CSS transitions, no `Math.random()` at runtime (use a seeded PRNG), and no `requestAnimationFrame` loops. Drift is a timeline tween too. Latin can be split per letter. **Arabic is never split into letters**, only words or lines (see §4).

### MG-201 Heat-cool word
A new word appears hot and cools to its final colour.
```js
// el = one word span (Latin: may be per-letter spans)
tl.fromTo(el,{opacity:0,scale:1.08,color:'#FFC560',textShadow:'0 0 28px rgba(255,120,30,.95)'},
  {opacity:1,scale:1,duration:0.12,ease:'power2.out'},T)
  .to(el,{color:'#FF6A22',duration:0.15,ease:'none'},T+0.12)
  .to(el,{color:'#EEE8F0',textShadow:'0 0 10px rgba(255,255,255,.25)',duration:0.3,ease:'power1.inOut'},T+0.27);
```
- The total is about 0.55 s, which matches the measured 30-36 f.
- If the word should stay as an accent, stop at `#FF5A1F`.
- For the white→ice look use `background:linear-gradient(90deg,#EEE8F0 55%,#BFE6F0)` with `-webkit-background-clip:text`. Animate a solid overlay copy's opacity instead of the gradient.

### MG-202 Narration line with a "current word" highlight
- The narration line sits small at top-left. Words appear in time with the voice.
- The current word is `#FF5A1F` and cools to `#EEE8F0` when the next word starts (`tl.to(prev,{color:'#EEE8F0',duration:.25},tNext)`).
- Unspoken words can be pre-shown at 25 % opacity in outline (`-webkit-text-stroke:1px`, `color:transparent`). This is what the source does: the next words "wait" as ghosts.

### MG-203 Per-letter slam title (Latin only)
- Letters drop in 3-4 f apart (`stagger:0.06`): `fromTo({y:-30,scale:1.4,opacity:0,filter:'blur(8px)'},{y:0,scale:1,opacity:1,filter:'blur(0)',duration:0.18,ease:'expo.out'})`.
- Combine with MG-201 so each letter is hot as it lands.
- The second line starts when the first line is about 70 % done.

### MG-204 Full-frame accent slam run (one word per hold)
- Background `#FF4A12` with a soft radial vignette. Text `#160B0C`, heavy extended caps, about 70 % of the frame width.
- For each word at time T:
  - Hide the previous word, but keep a ghost copy (opacity .4, `color:#B3200A`, `filter:blur(6px)`, `scale:1.15`) visible for 2 content frames (0.067 s).
  - The new word: `fromTo({scale:1.03},{scale:1,duration:0.17,ease:'expo.out'})`, with no fade. It is a hard cut plus a settle.
- Holds: 0.1-0.35 s per short word and about 0.7 s for the last word. The last word builds letter by letter (Latin) at 2 f/letter.
- Exit: the plane slides down `yPercent:100` in 0.05 s (`power4.in`).

### MG-205 Vertical tape-pull smear (exit)
- The outgoing layer gets `scaleY:1→2.2`, `y:-40%`, `opacity:1→0` over 0.1 s with `power3.in`, while 3 ghost copies at a −8/−16/−24 % y offset and decreasing opacity follow.
- Add `filter:blur(0→6px)`, vertical only. A shared SVG `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="0 12"` works.
- The incoming layer is already in place underneath.

### MG-206 Glitch re-frame (RGB double + slice)
- Over 4-6 f, duplicate the layer twice: a red copy `x:+8px` and a cyan copy `x:-8px`, each at opacity .5 with `mix-blend-mode:screen`.
- Clip 3-5 horizontal slices with `clip-path: inset()` and offset them by ±20-60 px. Step the offsets with `ease:'steps(3)'`.
- At the same time, jump the camera group to its new framing (scale/rotate).
- Use this to change the framing on a long hold every 1.5-2 s.

### MG-207 Whip-pan between panels on a big canvas
- Put all panels on one wide `#canvas` div. The camera is `#canvas` x/y/scale.
- Transition: `to('#canvas',{x:-panelX,duration:0.12,ease:'expo.inOut'})`, plus a horizontal blur (SVG `stdDeviation="18 0"`) whose strength follows the speed: `fromTo(blur,{v:0},{v:18,duration:.06,yoyo:true,repeat:1})`.

### MG-208 2.5D camera
- The stage wrapper has `perspective:1600px`, and the content plane uses `transformStyle:preserve-3d`.
- **Swing in**: `fromTo(plane,{rotationY:0},{rotationY:-28,rotationX:8,z:-120,duration:0.4,ease:'power3.inOut'})`.
- **Dolly**: `to(cam,{z:+400,duration:0.6,ease:'power2.in'})` into a target, then cut.
- **Constant drift on holds**: `scale 1→1.02` over the hold, `ease:'none'`.
- Add a floor: a CSS grid plane `rotateX(72deg)` with `background-image: linear-gradient` lines in `rgba(120,140,255,.12)`.

### MG-209 Light-trace border draw
- Use an SVG rect path with `pathLength=1`: `fromTo(path,{strokeDashoffset:1},{strokeDashoffset:0,duration:0.5,ease:'power2.inOut'})`.
- Add a 6 px orange `circle` riding the head, positioned with `MotionPathPlugin` on the same timeline. It must also be seekable.
- The stroke colour goes `#FF5A1F → rgba(220,225,240,.55)` over 0.4 s after the draw (a cooling trace).

### MG-210 Spark agent: travel / burst / ring
- **Spark**: an 8 px dot with `box-shadow:0 0 16px 6px rgba(255,90,30,.9)` and a 2-3 % scale pulse (yoyo).
- **Travel**: `MotionPath` along a wire, 0.4-0.6 s, `power2.inOut`.
- **Burst**: 24-40 particles from a seeded PRNG, each `fromTo({x:0,y:0,opacity:1},{x:cos*r,y:sin*r+g,opacity:0,duration:0.5-0.8,ease:'power3.out'})`.
- **Ring shockwave**: 3 circles, `scale 0→1`, `opacity 1→0`, staggered 0.08 s, 0.5 s each, `expo.out`.

### MG-211 Laser X / strike-through
- Two SVG lines draw from the corners in 0.35 s (`power2.in`), with spark heads (MG-210) and a small burst at each end.
- **Paper variant**: a red marker path with a hand wobble (pre-baked polyline with ±2 px noise from a seed), stroke 6 px `#D8302A`, opacity .9, drawn L→R over 0.6 s with `ease:'none'`.

### MG-212 Odometer digit roll
- Each digit is a column `0-9` in an `overflow:hidden` slot. `to(col,{yPercent:-10*target,duration:0.5,ease:'power3.out'})`.
- Stagger columns by 0.1 s. Underline the slot with an orange bar `scaleX 0→1`.
- **Arabic numerals** (٠-٩) or Latin digits both work.

### MG-213 Prompt-box typing with caret tracking
- Monospace text typed per character (Latin), at 30-45 chars/s, via a text span whose `textContent` comes from a `progress` proxy tweened on the timeline. That makes it seek-safe.
- The newest word has a `#FF5A1F` text colour plus a 3 px red bar under it, and cools when the next word starts.
- An orange block caret blinks with `steps(1)`, repeat yoyo, 0.5 s.
- The camera follows the caret x with a 0.2 s lag (`to(cam,{x:-caretX+W*0.6,ease:'power2.out'})` at each word).

### MG-214 Live-reactive demo (command → instant change)
For each command line typed in a chat panel:
1. Type it (MG-213).
2. Pause 0.15 s.
3. Make the visible change happen immediately in the preview: colour, shape morph, curve swap, loop icon.
4. Mark the line with a ✓ (MG-216).

This is the strongest "proof" device in the video. Use it to SHOW claims rather than state them.

### MG-215 Paper document inversion ("FORM")
- A hard cut to paper `#ECE7E2` with a subtle paper noise texture and a `#1C1C1C` header bar holding the title + mono metadata.
- Red typewriter text: per character for Latin, per word for Arabic.
- Flow boxes are dashed rectangles, built L→R with a camera pan. Then a red marker strike (MG-211) and a rotated rubber stamp slam: `fromTo({scale:2.2,opacity:0,rotation:-14},{scale:1,opacity:1,rotation:-8,duration:0.14,ease:'back.out(3)'})` plus a 2 px camera shake over 0.1 s.
- It feels like an official verdict and is the funny, human beat.

### MG-216 Checklist / questionnaire tick
- Checkboxes appear staggered by 0.08 s. The chosen box gets a red ✗ or ✓ drawn as an SVG path in 0.18 s.
- The other options dim to 40 %.

### MG-217 Outline → solid render scan
- Hold the element as an outline (`-webkit-text-stroke`, transparent fill / SVG stroke only).
- A 2 px red horizontal line sweeps top→bottom in 0.6 s. Above the line the solid copy is revealed with `clip-path: inset(0 0 ${100-p}% 0)`, using the same tween.

### MG-218 Film strip multiply
- Clone the preview frame 5-7 times into a horizontal strip with sprocket holes (repeating-linear-gradient).
- The strip x-scrolls 1.2 s with `ease:'none'` and a slight blur. Then the strip scales down and collapses into a single player frame (0.25 s, `expo.inOut`), and the play ▷ fills orange.

### MG-219 Field of many → wave away
- 300-600 small glyphs (keyframe diamonds) in a seeded grid. Individual ones flash orange on beats.
- Exit: a ripple `y:+60, rotationX:60, opacity:0`, staggered by distance from a point (`stagger:{from:[x,y],grid:'auto',amount:0.5}`).

### MG-220 Dictionary card (register change)
- Use a serif (Arabic: Amiri / Noto Naskh). Headword with syllable dots, part of speech in italic, numbered definitions typed.
- The headword's syllables fill orange in sequence. For Arabic, fill the whole word in 2-3 colour steps; never split it.

### MG-221 Loop ending
- End on the exact opening state (black + single spark dot) so the reel loops. Bridge with a 2-3 f white flash (`opacity 0→1→0`) from the last slam.

## 4. How to use it in Arabic talking-head reels

**Which beats to borrow as full-frame takeovers** (1-3 s each, 2-4 per reel, the speaker on screen the rest of the time):
1. **Slam run (MG-204)** for the punchline or "and this is where it gets crazy" moment. Use 3-6 short Arabic words in the profile's dialect, one per hold. Hard cuts with a ghost and a settle. This is the most re-usable beat.
2. **Prompt-box typing (MG-213)** whenever the speaker says what they typed to an AI tool. The prompt stays in its original language (often English mono), and the narration line above stays Arabic.
3. **Live-reactive demo (MG-214)** when the claim is "you just tell it and it changes". Show the command, then the instant change.
4. **FORM paper inversion (MG-215/216)** for a verdict or a myth-busting question (e.g. "does this replace tool X?" with three boxes: yes / no / not exactly, the last one ticked). It changes the palette and adds humour. Use it at most once per reel.
5. **Node diagram with a broken link (B6/B13, MG-210 + MG-211)** to explain "it doesn't do X directly, it goes through Y".
6. **Heat-cool title (MG-201)** for the name/product reveal (product name + version, with the digit roll, MG-212).

**Adapting Latin kinetic type to Arabic**
- Never split Arabic into letters, because letters join and break the word shaping. Every per-letter effect (MG-203, CRAZY letter-build, syllable fill) becomes a **word-level** effect:
  - Word slam with settle (MG-204).
  - An RTL clip-path wipe across the word: `clip-path: inset(0 0 0 100%)` → `inset(0)` over 0.15 s (reveal from the right).
  - Heat-cool on the whole word (MG-201).
- Narration-line words build **RTL**, anchored top-right. The current word is orange.
- The display font must be a heavy Arabic sans (Rubik 800-900 / Alexandria Black / IBM Plex Sans Arabic Bold). Avoid "extended" Latin-only fonts for the Arabic text.
- Mono stays Latin: prompts, code, HUD labels, `t = 0.00 s`. It reads as "tech" and avoids bad Arabic monospace.
- Ghost/smear effects (MG-205/206) are fine on Arabic because they move the whole layer.

**Palette (System B)**
- Dark stage `#0E0F16` + grid, one accent `#FF5A1F` (slam BG `#FF4A12`), text `#EEE8F0`. Paper inversion `#ECE7E2` / `#D8302A` only for the FORM beat.
- If the reel already uses System A, pick one system per video. Don't mix coral `#D26D5B` with this orange.

**Portrait conversion**
- The source is 16:10. In 9:16, stack the panels vertically: the narration line at top, the hero panel in the middle 60 %, the HUD/mono at the bottom.
- Whip-pans become vertical (MG-205/207 on y). Keep the takeovers outside the caption zone, or hide the captions during a takeover.
- 16:9 (1920×1080): keep text inside x 96–1824, y 54–1026; nothing important in the bottom 140 px (player controls).

**What NOT to take**
- The phone-filmed-monitor look: moiré, glare, the top light cone reflection, the browser player bar. Build the clean source instead.
- The static burned-in caption and the floating app logos.
- 44 s of continuous takeover. In a talking-head reel these are short cutaways, not the whole video.
- The heavy glow/bloom everywhere: keep it on titles only, it crushes on phones. Constant RGB glitches: at most one glitch re-frame per takeover.
- English slam words: translate to short words in the profile's dialect. Never stack more than about 6 slam holds (fatigue).
- Per-letter typing of Arabic.
